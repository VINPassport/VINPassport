Prove a vehicle's history.
Never show the record.

A Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport under Regulation (EU) 2026/1738, built on Midnight's zero-knowledge blockchain. Each audience sees only what it is entitled to, and checks it against the ledger itself.

1
The problem

A vehicle's history must be checkable by strangers,
and is too sensitive to publish

Today

The history a used-car buyer sees is whatever the seller chose to print. Write-offs re-registered as clean, accidents that vanish between listings, service stamps nobody earned: each is a field moving a way it should never move, and the buyer cannot tell.

The same is true of a boat, a motorbike or a motorhome sold privately, with a handshake and a folder of receipts.

From 29 November 2026, by law

Euro 7 puts an Environmental Vehicle Passport on every new M1/N1 type from 29 Nov 2026, all new M1/N1 a year later. It opens from a QR code on the car with no app and no login, and must stay reachable for twenty years (Reg (EU) 2025/1707 Art 17(4)). Art 4(7) separately obliges manufacturers to minimise tampering with the odometer and with the passport itself.

The fuller Digital Circularity Vehicle Passport follows in September 2032 (Reg (EU) 2026/1738 Art 13). 2032 is the roadmap. 29 November 2026 is the customer.

The law asks for a passport that can be checked, not one that is public. A full history is commercially sensitive to the seller and personally identifying to the owner. Publish it and one problem is solved by creating the other.

2
The insight

Prove claims about the record. Never publish the record.

Every field of the passport is a private commitment on Midnight. Questions are answered with zero-knowledge proofs (yes, verifiably) while the values stay where they belong.

The questionWhat the chain learns
Has it ever been written off?No, or the transaction fails
Has it had a reported accident?No, or the transaction fails
Record a service, a readingThe field moved only the way that field may move
Is the mileage under 150 000 km?Yes, and never the reading itself

Each field carries an integrity rule fixed at creation: mileage, accidents, keepers, write-off status can never fall. The rule lives on the ledger, so nobody can pick the flattering rule at the moment they need it.

What ships today

A working product, end to end

Our own Compact contract

  • 23 circuits: 18 provable, 5 pure helpers. Register, authorise and withdraw a writer, grant and revoke a view, initialise, record, three kinds of claim, six reads and the registrar change; the helpers derive slots and keys

How it works

Values stay with the writer. Only proofs travel.

6
The thirty seconds that carry the pitch

Type a lie into the form. Watch the circuit refuse it.

// 2016 Audi A4, the odometer rollback attempt
ok      update 1: odometerKm → 152 600
REFUSED update 2: odometerKm → 121 000: value decreased
ok      prove neverWrittenOff
REFUSED prove mileageUnder 150 000: value above the claimed bound
// refused steps wrote nothing, exactly as on chain

Why this matters

The refusal is not form validation: it is the zero-knowledge circuit aborting the transaction, so nothing is written and no false claim can ever appear proven.

The buyer's page then shows not proven for exactly that question: an honest absence, never a quiet "no".

Anyone can reproduce this: open passport.vin/intake/, pick the Audi from the demo fleet and submit. The circuits run in your browser. Or clone and npm run app.

The Wave 3 headline: tiered disclosure

One vehicle, three audiences. Each sees its tier, and checks it.

AudienceThe holder hands overWhat the reader's browser checks
Buyer · public tier3 fields and 4 proven claims, such as never written offEvery value against its ledger commitment; a claim counts only if current
Dealer · trade tier11 fields, for appraising a trade-inThe same, and whether the ledger holds a trade grant for this dealer
Authority · authority tier13 fields and the VINThe same, and the authority grant

Bounded by construction

Every one of the 68 fields carries a tier: 22 public, 41 trade, 5 authority. The builder refuses a field above the audience's tier; the reader recomputes the tiers and flags anything handed over that the audience was not entitled to. Change one value and the bundle fails.

Bundles the holder hands over, not a redaction server: nobody holds the full record for the reader's sake. The legal hook for different audiences seeing different things is Reg (EU) 2026/1738 Art 13(6)(d), conditions for access.

Try it, and what it answers

passport.vin/disclose/ checks all three on demo data, and bundles from the recorded Preview and preprod runs against contract state read from the public indexer.

What dealers gain (the Wave 2 reviewer's question): an honest dealer gets a clean-history proof it cannot fake today, the seller still chooses what to prove, and the trade view keeps the dealer's appraisal role. The dealers who lose are those whose margin depends on what the buyer cannot check.

8
Regulatory depth

The full DCVP panel, not a demo subset

32 provable field slots: 26 in use, 6 reserved (docs/FIELDS.md)

  • Fraud history: mileage, accidents, keepers, write-off, services: live fields under integrity rules
  • Art 13 identity: category, fuel type (BEV to diesel), type approval, emissions class, registration dates
  • Art 6 / 10 declarations: recycled plastic and the share of it from end-of-life vehicles; steel and aluminium slots held for the delegated acts that will set them; CO₂ footprint; dismantling evidence
  • New v retrofit: which path the passport came through, on the record (slot 30)

Where our passport ends: two boundaries

An EV's battery is a separate regime with its own passport (Reg (EU) 2023/1542, from Feb 2027). We carry the reference: slot 29, batteryPassportId, and never restate the battery's claims.

The Euro 7 EVP is also separate, and we read its parameter table rather than assume it. Appendix 1 to Annex V is about twenty type-approval and emissions values, fixed at approval. We hold two, and the odometer is not an EVP parameter at all: it reaches us through Art 4(7)(d) anti-tampering and the Art 4(10) lifetime-values regime.

Both boundaries get one answer: interoperate, do not absorb. Separate section roots under one top root, because they are different documents on different clocks.

Every declaration anchors under a salted content root: it commits to everything and reveals nothing, including which slots are filled.

Quality and reliability

Tested like it matters, honest about what a proof means

The numbers, copied from the output

176contract tests, run against the compiled circuits
388app tests
24SDK assumption checks
  • Mutation-checked: every guard deleted in turn, recompiled, re-run: 43 of 43 killed
  • Reproducible: CI recompiles from a hash-checked compiler and fails on any changed committed byte, verifier keys included
  • Read back from the chain: each run is checked against its receipt through the public indexer (13 of 13 on the live Preview contract, events included), and no VIN or salt is found there
  • The site checks itself: check:a11y drives all 15 pages in a real browser (keyboard, focus, 320 to 1920 px, reduced motion); check:facts ties every figure on the site and in this deck to its source. Lighthouse, home page, mobile: 95 · 100 · 100 · 100

Measured, not assumed

Proving, wasm, in-process: register 17.5s, at-most 27.6s, at-least 27.0s, initialise a field 37.4s. Paid once by the writer; a verifier reads the verdict off the ledger and never waits on a proof.

In-circuit ECDSA registrar keys work but prove about seven times slower (167s against 24s), so they wait for a manufacturer that needs keys held in a hardware security module.

What we do not claim

A proof shows a value is the anchored one and satisfies a bound: not that it matched physical reality when written. The registrar is trusted for that, which is why every passport names one, and why the ledger records who wrote what.

So VINPassport today makes fraud attributable and tamper-evident: never "impossible". Closing the last gap means the value is written by a party already accountable for it: work we have not done, and do not imply we have.

Business: how this earns

Mandated demand, fiat-only revenue, free verification

The model

  • Who pays first: the manufacturer, once per vehicle, at issue. Euro 7 Art 7(4) obliges it to issue one, 2025/1707 Art 17(4) to keep it reachable for 20 years. It buys a record anyone can check is the one it issued, reachable whether or not we are
  • A second product: the service and ownership history the regulated passport does not hold, paid for by second-hand dealers, who also get the trade view. A hypothesis until a dealer says yes
  • Who answers for a value: whoever wrote it. The ledger names the identity behind every write; the contract refuses a rollback but does not vouch for truth
  • Reading is free, always: the law forbids asking a reader to register (2025/1707 Annex V 4.2.3). Every scanned code is a buyer learning to demand proof
  • Every payment is fiat. No customer holds crypto or pays a network fee: we pay in DUST from NIGHT we hold

Why the demand is not speculative

From 29 November 2026 every new M1/N1 type needs an Environmental Vehicle Passport (Euro 7); the battery passport follows in February 2027, the full passport in September 2032. Second-hand dealers are stage 3, inheriting passports rather than adopting a new system.

Lifestyle vehicles launch with cars and vans

Boats, motorbikes, quads and e-bikes need no vehicle passport by law (an e-bike's battery gets its own from Feb 2027, Reg 2023/1542 Art 77), so they buy on resale. A verified history makes a vehicle easier to sell (randomised auction trial, Tadelis and Zettelmeyer, AER 2015).

Cost, measured on chain

Registering a vehicle cost 0.60 DUST and each later write about 0.32 (Preview, 9 Nov, with events). NIGHT is never spent: the wallet projects about 61 DUST an hour from 5,000 held NIGHT. A fee is cost times the price of the moment, so mainnet's will differ. The larger cost is proving, paid once at issue (slide 10).
