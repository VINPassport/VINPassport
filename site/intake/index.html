Step 04

Record at registration

rule: never falls

Each field is created under its integrity rule, fixed on the ledger at creation. None of these may ever fall. Keepers 0 is what says new, never registered. The last three are pairs: the odometer against the reading at the last service, and recalls issued against recalls completed, which is what lets a claim compare two hidden values without publishing either.

Step 05

Claims to prove

from the published catalogue

A claim the record cannot support aborts during proving and writes nothing. Verification then shows not proven, never a false tick. The list is the published catalogue, narrowed to the questions this form collects the fields for: the console cannot offer a question it has no way to answer.

Step 06

Who else may write this vehicle

authoriseWriter

A car outlives the party that registered it. Hand the right to write this one vehicle to a dealership or a garage, and take it back later. The integrity rule does not move with it: an authorised writer can raise the odometer and still cannot lower it.

Only the registrar of a vehicle may authorise a writer for it, and the circuit checks that rather than trusting the console. No secret leaves your machine: the page sends a name, and the identity behind it is derived in-circuit from a secret this process holds.
